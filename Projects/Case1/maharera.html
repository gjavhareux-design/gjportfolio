📄 Lean UX Step 0101

Project Context & Case Study Setup

Define the product story before showing the process.

🎯 Purpose

A UX case study needs a clear frame: what problem existed, who was affected, what your role was, what constraints shaped the work, and what outcome the team pursued.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Write a short product overview: product name, domain, platform, audience, and business context.
  2. State the challenge in one concise paragraph without jumping to a solution.
  3. Clarify your role and contributions across research, design, facilitation, testing, and delivery.
  4. List the team members and stakeholders involved in decisions.
  5. Document constraints such as timeline, budget, technical limits, compliance, accessibility, brand rules, and data availability.
  6. Define what success would look like from user, business, and technical perspectives.
  7. Set boundaries: what was in scope, out of scope, and deferred.
What to explainExplain why the project mattered, why the problem was worth solving, what you owned personally, and what limitations shaped the process.

Artifacts to include

Project briefRole matrixTimelineScope mapSuccess criteria

Key questions

  • Why did this project matter?
  • Who was the user?
  • What was the business opportunity or risk?
  • What did I personally contribute?

💡 Lean UX Step 0202

Assumptions, Risks & Proto-Personas

Make team beliefs visible before investing in solutions.

🎯 Purpose

Lean UX begins by exposing assumptions. Instead of writing large requirement documents upfront, the team captures what it believes to be true and identifies which beliefs must be validated quickly.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Run an assumptions workshop with product, design, engineering, support, and stakeholders.
  2. Ask everyone to write assumptions about users, needs, behaviours, barriers, channels, business value, feasibility, and risks.
  3. Cluster similar assumptions into themes such as user needs, market context, adoption risks, and technical dependencies.
  4. Convert the riskiest assumptions into testable hypotheses.
  5. Create proto-personas using current knowledge: goals, behaviours, pains, context, triggers, tools, and decision criteria.
  6. Rank assumptions by uncertainty and impact.
  7. Document what evidence supports each assumption and what evidence is missing.
What to explainShow that the team did not pretend to know everything. Explain which assumptions were risky and how you chose what to test first.

Artifacts to include

Assumption mapRisk matrixProto-personasHypothesis backlogEvidence tracker

Key questions

  • What are we assuming users need?
  • Which assumption is most dangerous if wrong?
  • What evidence do we already have?
  • What should we test first?

🎯 Lean UX Step 0303

Business Outcomes & User Outcomes

Shift from output thinking to outcome thinking.

🎯 Purpose

A strong Lean UX case study shows that the team did not simply ship screens. It explains the user behaviour the team wanted to change and how that behaviour supports business value.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Define business outcomes such as increased activation, reduced support calls, higher completion rate, better retention, or improved conversion.
  2. Define user outcomes such as confidence, independence, faster task completion, fewer errors, or greater trust.
  3. Connect business outcomes to user outcomes so the story is not purely commercial or purely aesthetic.
  4. Identify leading indicators and lagging indicators.
  5. Avoid vanity metrics and focus on meaningful behaviour.
  6. Create a measurement plan before design work begins.
  7. Agree on baseline data or define how the baseline will be collected.
What to explainClarify what success meant before you designed. Show the relationship between user benefit and business value.

Artifacts to include

Outcome statementMetric treeKPI baselineMeasurement planNorth Star metric

Key questions

  • What behaviour are we trying to change?
  • How will we know the solution worked?
  • What metric could mislead us?
  • What baseline do we need?

👥 Lean UX Step 0404

Discovery Research

Learn enough to reduce uncertainty, not everything possible.

🎯 Purpose

Lean UX research is lightweight but rigorous. The goal is to gather enough evidence to guide the next experiment and prevent the team from building the wrong thing.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Create a research plan with objectives, target participants, method, recruitment criteria, script, timeline, and analysis approach.
  2. Choose methods based on uncertainty: interviews for motivations, usability tests for interactions, surveys for broader signals, analytics for behaviour, and support logs for pain points.
  3. Recruit participants who match the audience, including edge cases and accessibility needs where relevant.
  4. Conduct interviews focused on past behaviour rather than imagined future preferences.
  5. Capture direct quotes, observed behaviours, workarounds, emotional moments, decision triggers, and unmet needs.
  6. Review existing data such as funnels, support tickets, app reviews, sales notes, CRM data, and search logs.
  7. Synthesise findings into themes, opportunities, and evidence-backed problem statements.
What to explainDo not only say “we interviewed users.” Explain who you spoke to, why, what you learned, and how it changed the direction.

Artifacts to include

Research planInterview scriptParticipant screenerAffinity mapInsight reportQuote bank

Key questions

  • What do users actually do today?
  • Where do they struggle?
  • What triggers the need?
  • What language do users use?

🗺️ Lean UX Step 0505

Problem Framing & Opportunity Definition

Turn research into a sharp design challenge.

🎯 Purpose

Before ideating, the team must align on the real problem. This section shows how raw observations became focused opportunities.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Summarise research into key insights supported by quotes, observations, or data.
  2. Create journey maps showing stages, touchpoints, pain points, emotions, questions, and opportunities.
  3. Write problem statements that identify user, need, context, and barrier.
  4. Use “How Might We” questions to reframe problems into opportunity areas.
  5. Prioritise opportunities by impact, confidence, effort, risk, and strategic alignment.
  6. Identify moments that matter — the points where intervention can create the largest improvement.
  7. Confirm stakeholder alignment before moving into solution exploration.
What to explainExplain why this was the right problem to solve now and what you intentionally chose not to solve.

Artifacts to include

Insight cardsJourney mapProblem statementHMW listOpportunity grid

Key questions

  • What is the root problem?
  • Which pain point matters most?
  • Which opportunity is valuable and feasible?
  • What should not be solved right now?

✨ Lean UX Step 0606

Collaborative Ideation

Generate many possible solutions before choosing one.

🎯 Purpose

Lean UX works best when ideation is collaborative. Designers do not disappear and return with a finished answer; the team explores options together.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Invite a cross-functional group so ideas include user value, feasibility, business needs, and operational realities.
  2. Start with selected “How Might We” questions.
  3. Use time-boxed methods such as Crazy 8s, design studio, mind mapping, storyboarding, or solution sketching.
  4. Encourage quantity first, then evaluate quality later.
  5. Ask participants to explain assumptions behind ideas.
  6. Cluster ideas into themes such as guidance, automation, simplification, education, navigation, trust-building, and error prevention.
  7. Vote or score ideas against desirability, viability, feasibility, accessibility, and risk reduction.
What to explainShow the breadth of explored options and why the selected direction was strongest.

Artifacts to include

SketchesIdea wallDesign studio outputsVoting resultsStoryboards

Key questions

  • What are all the ways we could solve this?
  • Which ideas test the riskiest hypothesis?
  • Which ideas are small enough to learn from quickly?
  • What trade-offs exist?

🧪 Lean UX Step 0707

Hypotheses & Experiment Planning

Convert ideas into testable learning loops.

🎯 Purpose

Each design decision should connect to a hypothesis and an experiment that can prove or disprove it. This creates the Lean UX build-measure-learn loop.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Write hypotheses using: “We believe [user type] needs [capability] because [insight]. We will know this is true when [measurable outcome].”
  2. Define the riskiest assumption each hypothesis addresses.
  3. Choose the smallest experiment that can generate useful evidence.
  4. Select experiment types such as paper prototype, clickable prototype, concierge test, Wizard-of-Oz test, landing page test, usability test, A/B test, survey, fake-door test, or analytics review.
  5. Define participants, tasks, success criteria, data to collect, and decision rules.
  6. Set learning goals before building the prototype.
  7. Create an experiment backlog and prioritise by risk, impact, and speed.
What to explainDescribe what result would change your mind. This shows maturity and avoids confirmation bias.

Artifacts to include

Hypothesis cardsExperiment planLearning backlogTest scriptDecision criteria

Key questions

  • What do we need to learn next?
  • What is the cheapest way to learn it?
  • What result would change our mind?
  • What will we do if the hypothesis fails?

✏️ Lean UX Step 0808

MVP, Prototype & Design Exploration

Build only what is needed to learn.

🎯 Purpose

In Lean UX, prototypes are learning tools. They should be realistic enough to test the hypothesis but not so polished that the team becomes attached too early.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Start with sketches or wireframes to explore structure, content, flow, and priority.
  2. Map key user flows from entry point to desired outcome, including empty states, error states, edge cases, and recovery paths.
  3. Create clickable prototypes when interaction flow matters.
  4. Increase fidelity only when visual hierarchy, trust, brand perception, or detailed interaction behaviour must be tested.
  5. Use design system components where possible to reduce implementation waste.
  6. Define the MVP as the smallest version that delivers user value and generates learning.
  7. Annotate design decisions with the insight, assumption, or hypothesis they support.
What to explainShow why you selected the prototype fidelity and how it matched the learning objective.

Artifacts to include

User flowInformation architectureWireframesClickable prototypeMVP scope

Key questions

  • What is the minimum thing we need to build?
  • Which parts need fidelity?
  • What edge cases could break the experience?
  • What can be faked for learning?

✅ Lean UX Step 0909

Usability Testing & Validation

Observe behaviour, not just opinions.

🎯 Purpose

Validation reveals whether the design helps users achieve the intended outcome. It exposes usability issues, comprehension gaps, trust problems, and mismatches between expectations and product behaviour.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Prepare a test plan with research goals, participant criteria, tasks, prototype links, success metrics, and facilitator notes.
  2. Use realistic scenarios instead of leading instructions.
  3. Ask participants to think aloud, but prioritise observed behaviour over stated preference.
  4. Track task success, time on task, error rate, hesitation, misclicks, comprehension, confidence, and qualitative comments.
  5. Avoid defending the design during sessions. Ask neutral follow-up questions.
  6. Debrief after each session and capture issues while evidence is fresh.
  7. Rate issues by severity, frequency, and impact on the target outcome.
What to explainShow what failed as well as what worked. Strong case studies demonstrate learning, not perfection.

Artifacts to include

Test planTask scriptSession notesIssue logSeverity matrixValidation report

Key questions

  • Can users complete the task?
  • Where do they hesitate?
  • What do they misunderstand?
  • Does the design support the target behaviour?

📊 Lean UX Step 1010

Measure, Learn & Decide

Turn evidence into product decisions.

🎯 Purpose

Lean UX is not complete when testing ends. The team must interpret evidence and decide whether to persevere, pivot, iterate, narrow scope, or stop.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Compare results against the success criteria defined in the experiment plan.
  2. Separate validated learning from opinions, preferences, and isolated anecdotes.
  3. Look for patterns across research, analytics, support data, and stakeholder feedback.
  4. Update hypothesis status: validated, invalidated, partially validated, or inconclusive.
  5. Decide the next action: iterate, run another experiment, narrow scope, pivot, or move into delivery.
  6. Document what changed because of the evidence.
  7. Share a concise learning report with stakeholders.
What to explainMake the decision logic visible. Hiring managers and stakeholders want to see how evidence changed the work.

Artifacts to include

Learning reportExperiment resultsDashboardDecision logUpdated backlog

Key questions

  • What did we learn?
  • What evidence supports the decision?
  • What remains uncertain?
  • What should change next?

🔁 Lean UX Step 1111

Iteration & Design Refinement

Improve the solution based on evidence.

🎯 Purpose

Iteration is where Lean UX becomes visible. A strong case study shows how the design changed, why it changed, and what evidence informed each revision.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Prioritise usability issues and improvements by severity, outcome impact, effort, and confidence.
  2. Revise flows, screens, content, states, navigation, hierarchy, and interaction patterns.
  3. Create before-and-after comparisons to show design evolution.
  4. Update accessibility considerations such as keyboard navigation, contrast, focus states, labels, errors, touch targets, and screen reader support.
  5. Review feasibility with engineering before finalising refined designs.
  6. Run follow-up tests if changes affect critical assumptions or high-risk interactions.
  7. Maintain clear rationale for every major design change.
What to explainDo not hide earlier versions. Showing how the design improved makes your process more credible.

Artifacts to include

Iteration logBefore/after mockupsRevised prototypeAccessibility checklistDesign rationale

Key questions

  • What changed after testing?
  • Why is the new version better?
  • Did the fix introduce new problems?
  • Is the design ready for delivery?

🚀 Lean UX Step 1212

Delivery, Handoff & Implementation Support

Translate validated design into buildable product work.

🎯 Purpose

Lean UX does not end with prototypes. The design must be implemented with quality, clarity, and alignment across product, engineering, and design.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Prepare production-ready designs using design system components and responsive layouts.
  2. Document flows, states, rules, content, microcopy, accessibility requirements, analytics events, and acceptance criteria.
  3. Define empty states, loading states, error states, permission issues, offline states, validation errors, and partial completion.
  4. Hold handoff sessions with engineers and product managers.
  5. Support implementation through design QA, sprint reviews, and bug triage.
  6. Check analytics instrumentation against the measurement plan.
  7. Create release notes or internal enablement material where needed.
What to explainShow that you can move from design thinking to practical delivery without losing quality.

Artifacts to include

Final UIDesign specComponentsAcceptance criteriaAnalytics mapQA checklist

Key questions

  • Can engineering build this clearly?
  • Are all states specified?
  • How will we measure launch impact?
  • What quality checks are needed?

⏱️ Lean UX Step 1313

Launch, Monitoring & Continuous Learning

Measure real-world impact and keep learning.

🎯 Purpose

After launch, real usage data reveals whether the solution creates intended outcomes at scale. This section closes the loop with impact and future improvements.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Monitor agreed metrics after release and compare them to baseline.
  2. Review qualitative feedback from users, support, sales, app reviews, surveys, or customer success teams.
  3. Track adoption, completion, retention, drop-off, errors, support contacts, satisfaction, and accessibility feedback.
  4. Identify unexpected behaviours, new pain points, and technical issues.
  5. Run post-launch experiments if uncertainty remains.
  6. Document what improved, what did not, and why.
  7. Define next iteration opportunities and add them to the product backlog.
What to explainConnect launch data back to the original outcomes. If results are mixed, say so honestly.

Artifacts to include

Launch dashboardPost-launch reportFeedback themesImpact summaryNext backlog

Key questions

  • Did the solution achieve the outcome?
  • What changed in real behaviour?
  • What surprised us?
  • What should we improve next?

💬 Lean UX Step 1414

Case Study Results & Reflection

End with impact, learning, and maturity.

🎯 Purpose

A memorable UX case study explains not only what was designed, but what was learned, what trade-offs were made, and how the work improved the product or organisation.

🧩 Step-by-step activities

  1. Summarise measurable outcomes, qualitative outcomes, and business impact.
  2. Show the final solution and connect it back to the original problem.
  3. Explain key trade-offs, constraints, and difficult decisions.
  4. Reflect on what worked well and what you would improve next time.
  5. Highlight how product, engineering, stakeholders, and users influenced the result.
  6. Explain limitations honestly if data was incomplete, sample size was small, or metrics were still maturing.
  7. Close with next steps and future opportunities.
What to explainEnd with confidence and honesty. Show impact, but also show what you learned as a designer.

Artifacts to include

Key questions

  • What was the result?
  • What did I learn?
  • What would I do differently?
  • What is the next opportunity?
